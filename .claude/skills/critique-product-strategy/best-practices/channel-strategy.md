# Growth / channel strategy

The growth strategy addresses **how we will attract our target audience to our product, and how we'll do so sustainably**.

## What great looks like
- Names **1–2 primary channels** that fit where the target audience already spends time (e.g., content/SEO, paid acquisition, partnerships, sales, referrals, app stores, community, product-led virality).
- **Channel-audience fit:** there's evidence the audience can be reached there.
- **Unit economics:** customer acquisition cost (CAC) is plausibly well below customer lifetime value (LTV), commonly LTV:CAC ≥ 3.
- **Sustainable:** includes a compounding loop (referrals, content, network effects, user-generated content), not only paid spend.
- **Sequenced:** how we get the first 100 / 1,000 customers vs. how we scale.
- **Channel-product fit:** the product's price and complexity match the channel (e.g., a low-price product can't support direct sales).

## Red flags to call out
- "Go viral", "word of mouth", or "marketing" with no mechanism behind it.
- Listing every channel with no priority.
- Channel doesn't reach the stated audience, or is saturated and expensive.
- CAC ignored, or clearly incompatible with the price point.
- No plan for the first customers.
- Growth depends on retention the product hasn't shown.

## Questions to ask
- Where will the first 100 customers come from, specifically?
- What does it cost to acquire a customer, and how long until they pay it back?
- What makes growth compound rather than scale linearly with spend?
