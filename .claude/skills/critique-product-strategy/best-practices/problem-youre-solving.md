# Problem you're solving

A great problem statement describes an **acute, frequent (or high-stakes), and evidenced** problem in the customer's life, deeply enough to guide the solution.

## What great looks like
- Framed from the **customer's point of view** (their situation, goal, and obstacle), not as a missing feature.
- **Acute:** it's painful enough that customers actively seek a solution, spend money, or build workarounds.
- **Frequency or stakes are clear:** it happens often, or the cost of getting it wrong is high.
- **Current alternatives and workarounds are described**, and so is why they fall short.
- **Root cause understood:** why the problem exists, not just its symptoms.
- **Evidence:** customer interviews, quotes, data, or observed behavior, not assertion.

## Red flags to call out
- A "nice-to-have": the problem is real, but people don't care enough to act.
- Problem stated as the absence of our solution ("people lack an app that…").
- Sweeping claims ("most people…", "everyone struggles…") with no evidence.
- Episodic or rare problem with no plan for ongoing engagement.
- Several problems bundled together without a clear primary one.
- The problem isn't felt most by the stated target audience.

## Questions to ask
- When did a customer last experience this, and what did they do?
- What does the problem cost them (money, time, stress, missed outcomes)?
- What evidence shows this is a top-3 problem for them, not a top-20 problem?
