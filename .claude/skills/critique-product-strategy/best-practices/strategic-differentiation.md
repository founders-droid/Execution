# Strategic differentiation (how to endure)

Strategic differentiation answers: **why will we keep winning despite fast-moving competition?** A good value proposition wins customers today; differentiation protects that win once competitors copy it.

## What great looks like
- Names **durable advantages (moats)** that strengthen over time, e.g.:
  - **Network effects:** the product gets better as more people use it.
  - **Proprietary data:** usage creates data competitors can't get.
  - **Switching costs:** customers' data, workflows, or integrations make leaving painful.
  - **Scale economies:** unit costs fall faster than competitors' do.
  - **Brand and trust:** especially in high-stakes categories.
  - **Unique assets:** distribution, partnerships, regulatory position, technology.
- Explains **how the moat is built step by step**, starting from zero customers.
- Shows **honest awareness of competitors**, both current and likely entrants, and how they'd respond.

## Red flags to call out
- "Better UX", "first mover", or "we'll execute faster" as the only advantage.
- An advantage an incumbent or a well-funded startup could copy in months.
- Features presented as a moat.
- Out-of-date or incomplete competitor landscape; missing the closest direct competitors.
- The moat only exists at a scale the strategy has no plan to reach.

## Questions to ask
- If a well-funded competitor copied our product tomorrow, why would customers stay?
- Which advantage gets stronger with each new customer?
- Who are the 3 closest competitors today, and what will they do in response?
