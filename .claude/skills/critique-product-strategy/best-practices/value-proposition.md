# Value proposition

The value proposition is **our unique solution to the customer's problem**. Since there are many ways to solve a given problem, a great strategy is clear about its *specific approach* and why that approach is better for this audience.

## What great looks like
- States the **outcome for the customer**, not a feature list.
- Names the **specific approach** and why it beats the alternatives (including "do nothing" and DIY workarounds).
- **Directly addresses the stated problem** for the stated audience.
- Clearly **better on the dimension the customer cares most about** (often an order of magnitude better, not marginally better).
- Believable and **testable**: it's clear how we'd know customers value it.

## Red flags to call out
- A list of features with no customer outcome.
- Generic claims ("easy, powerful, all-in-one", "AI-powered") that any competitor could make.
- Solves a different problem than the one stated.
- Only marginally better than alternatives: not enough to make customers switch.
- Ignores switching costs or the effort for customers to adopt.
- The claimed value depends on unproven technical or data capabilities.

## Questions to ask
- In one sentence, why would a target customer choose this over what they use today?
- What must be true for the value to be delivered, and is that proven?
- What is the customer's "aha" moment, and how fast do they get there?
