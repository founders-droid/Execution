# Business model / monetization strategy

The business model explains **how we will generate revenue, the specific pricing strategy, and how that leads to a profitable business**.

## What great looks like
- **Clear revenue model:** subscription, usage-based, transaction fee, freemium, advertising, marketplace take rate, licensing, etc.
- **Who pays** (the user, their employer, a third party) and **why they'll pay** — tied to the value proposition.
- **Pricing and packaging** stated, with a rationale: value-based, and benchmarked against competitors and alternatives.
- **Willingness to pay** evidenced (surveys, pre-orders, pricing tests, comparable products).
- **Unit economics path to profitability:** gross margin, retention/churn, LTV vs. CAC, payback period.
- **Aligned incentives:** how we make money doesn't undermine customer trust.

## Red flags to call out
- "We'll figure out monetization later" with no hypothesis.
- Price chosen with no link to value or competitive alternatives.
- Revenue model conflicts with the audience (e.g., a premium subscription for a price-sensitive segment) or with the channel's CAC.
- Model depends on high retention for an infrequently used product.
- Conflicts of interest (e.g., earning commissions on products you recommend) that damage trust.
- Profitability depends on unrealistic scale or margins.

## Questions to ask
- What will a customer pay, how often, and what evidence shows they will?
- What are the LTV, CAC, and payback period, even as rough estimates?
- How does revenue grow per customer over time?
