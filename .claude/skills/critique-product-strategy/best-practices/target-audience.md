# Target audience

A great target audience is **specific, underserved, and reachable**, with the potential to become raving fans.

## What great looks like
- **Specific segment**, defined by shared needs and behaviors, not just demographics (e.g., "dual-income parents who already track spending in spreadsheets," not "millennials").
- **A beachhead (first target segment) is chosen.** One primary segment first; other segments are explicitly sequenced for later.
- **Underserved:** current solutions fail this segment in a specific, nameable way.
- **Acute pain + ability to pay + reachable** through identifiable channels.
- **Sized:** a rough estimate of how many such customers exist and whether that's enough to matter.
- **Evidence:** interviews, data, or observed behavior show the segment exists and cares.

## Red flags to call out
- "Everyone" or a broad demographic as the audience.
- Several segments treated as equal priorities with no beachhead.
- The segment is well served by existing solutions, so why would they switch?
- No evidence the segment feels the pain.
- The segment can't be reached cost-effectively, or can't or won't pay.
- The audience doesn't match the problem, value proposition, or pricing described elsewhere in the strategy.

## Questions to ask
- Who exactly is the first customer, and why them before anyone else?
- What do they use today, and what specifically fails them?
- How many of them are there, and how will we find them?
