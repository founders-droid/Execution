# What great product strategy looks like

Before critiquing a strategy, it's important to understand what great strategy looks like in the first place.

A comprehensive product strategy needs to address these 3 high-level questions:

1. **Where to play:** what market are we building our products and services in?
2. **How to win:** why will our products and services win over customers relative to the other competitors in the space?
3. **How to endure:** why will our product continue to win the market despite fast-moving competition?

```
Product strategy
├── Where to play
│   ├── Problem to solve
│   └── Target audience
├── How to win
│   ├── Value proposition
│   ├── Business model
│   └── Growth strategy
└── How to endure
    └── Strategic differentiation
```

Let's now break down each of these questions into their critical dimensions.

## Where to play
When it comes to defining the market we play in, there are two critical dimensions we need to answer.

**The problem we are solving.** Here we want to deeply understand and articulate the acute problem that arises in our customer's lives for which we are offering a solution. This deep understanding will help us craft the best solution to their challenges. → see `problem-youre-solving.md`

**The target audience.** Here we want to be crisp on the specific customer segments we are building our product for that will get the most value out of our solution. There are inevitably multiple target audiences we could go after, so the work becomes finding the underserved segments that have the potential to become raving fans. → see `target-audience.md`

## How to win
When it comes to the question of how to win, there are 3 critical levers we have for winning customers relative to the competition.

**The value proposition.** This is the value we will deliver to our customers. Said another way, it's our unique solution to the customer's problem. Given there are often many ways to solve a given problem, we need to be clear about our specific approach. → see `value-proposition.md`

**The business model.** This gets at how we will generate revenue for the business, the specific pricing strategy we will pursue, and how that will ultimately lead to a profitable business. → see `monetization-strategy.md`

**The growth strategy.** This dimension addresses how we will attract our target audience to our products and services, and how we will do so in a sustainable way. → see `channel-strategy.md`

## How to endure
Winning customers today isn't enough. Competitors will copy what works. The strategy must explain what advantage compounds over time and is hard to copy.

**Strategic differentiation.** The durable advantages (moats) that let us keep winning despite fast-moving competition. → see `strategic-differentiation.md`

## How to use this in a critique
- A strategy that skips any of the 6 dimensions is incomplete. Call out the gap explicitly.
- The dimensions must fit together: the value proposition must solve *the stated problem* for *the stated audience*, the channel must reach *that* audience, the business model must match what *that* audience will pay, and the differentiation must protect *that* value proposition.
- Inconsistencies between dimensions are often the most important critique.
